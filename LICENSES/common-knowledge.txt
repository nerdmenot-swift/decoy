No licence text exists for this source, and that is a recorded conclusion
rather than an omission.

This source covers the categories every faker library ships and Decoy
refused for months: animals, birds, dog and cat breeds, fruit and
vegetables, cheeses, dishes, mountains, rivers, trees, flowers,
gemstones, book titles and authors, genres, instruments, philosophers,
scientists, composers, artists, explorers, a small set of living public
figures, three groups of trademarked brands, and universities, football
clubs and museums.

## Why there is no grant to reproduce

Nobody owns these facts. That an otter is an animal, that Kilimanjaro is
a mountain, that Vermeer painted, that Ajax is a football club -- none of
these is anyone's authorship, and no licence is needed to state them.

What can be owned is a particular *compilation*: somebody's selection and
arrangement of facts, which is why this project refuses Wikipedia-derived
datasets that arrive relabelled as CC0. No such compilation was copied
here. The lists were written from general knowledge for this project, and
their selection and arrangement are Decoy's own, covered by the Apache
License, Version 2.0 in LICENSE at the root of this repository.

## What is being given up

Every other source in this corpus is fetched from a pinned URL and
verified against an integrity hash, so an upstream that changes silently
fails the build. This one cannot be. There is no upstream, so there is
nothing to hash and nothing to re-verify, and the accuracy of the content
rests on having been written carefully rather than on having been
checked.

That is a weaker guarantee than anything else here offers, and it is the
reason this is a separate source rather than more rows under
`decoy-authored`. `decoy-authored` claims to be original content that
cannot be wrong because there is no fact of the matter -- an invented pub
name is neither correct nor incorrect. These lists are the opposite: they
are entirely facts, so they can be wrong, and no part of this pipeline
would notice if one were.

Errors here are expected to be rare and cosmetic. They are also fixable
in a diff, which is the same guarantee `decoy-authored` offers and a
better one than a hash over somebody else's server.

## Trademarks

Camera, phone and appliance manufacturers appear by name, as do
universities, football clubs and museums. Naming a trademark to refer to
the thing it names is nominative use, and is not what trademark law
restricts -- the restriction is on using a mark so as to suggest
endorsement, sponsorship or affiliation, or to cause confusion in trade.
A fixture generator does none of that. Decoy is not affiliated with, and
claims no endorsement from, any organisation named in this source.

## Living people

`notable.actor`, `notable.musician` and `notable.athlete` name people who
are alive. Decoy refuses rosters of real people, and declined a national
election-candidate database on exactly that ground, so the distinction
being drawn is worth stating.

That database identifies private individuals: ordinary people, listed
with a constituency and a party affiliation, most of whom are not public
figures in any meaningful sense. These lists name people whose names are
already universally known, and pair them with nothing -- no address, no
date of birth, no employer -- so no generated record resembles a record
*about* the person named. The lists are kept short for the same reason;
the volume in this namespace is in the historical figures, who raise no
such question at all.

Anyone named here who would rather not be may have the entry removed on
request.
